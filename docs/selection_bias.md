# Selection and reject inference

These functions distinguish the target population of applicants from the population whose own funded contract has an observed outcome. Acceptance, take-up, funding and observation are different selection mechanisms. Their outputs do not turn a declined applicant's unobserved contract into a measured good or bad loan. A bureau outcome on another contract remains a proxy with a different evidence scope.

All calculations return `CalcResult`; the two fitting functions return `FitResult` containing a portable common `ModelArtifact`. `context` is the optional common calculation context. Arrays use input order. Fold indices are zero-based in both languages. Python missing outcomes are `None` (numeric NaN is also accepted for unobserved rows); native R uses a list containing `NULL`, or a numeric vector with NA for unobserved rows. Outcomes on observed rows must be numeric zero or one. Supplying a label on an unobserved row raises `UNOBSERVED_OUTCOME`.

## Dated observation and propensity contracts

`build_selection_dataset(applications, decisions, bookings, outcomes, *, as_of, target_population, target, context=None)` joins unique `application_id` records. `applications` require `entity_id` and `submitted_at`. The grouping identifier should identify the independent person/household, including repeat applications. All stage records require event date `as_of` and information date `known_at`. Information cannot precede the event. Stage events must follow submission and earlier observed stages. Information arriving after the audit `as_of` cutoff is excluded.

A decision has `decision` equal to `offered`, `rejected`, `withdrawn` or `not_decisionable`, and a nonempty `policy_version`. A booking contains boolean `taken_up` and `funded`; funding requires take-up and a recorded offer. An own-contract outcome has `outcome_scope="own_contract"`, `status` (`observed`, `immature`, `censored`), the exact `target`, and a binary `outcome` only if observed. The caller must construct the mature/censored target using the declared horizon and exit policy before joining. The function preserves that distinction; it does not infer maturity from a default-free partial history. External-contract rows use `outcome_scope="external_contract"` and are retained separately, never copied into the own-contract target. `target_population` is an explicit textual population definition. `target` declares event, positive horizon, days/months/years, PHYSICAL/RISK_NEUTRAL and conditioning. Results include the panel and counts by distinct stage strata.

`fit_selection_propensity(X, selected, *, feature_order, mechanism, folds, row_metadata, target, training, model_id, model_version, context=None)` fits an actual Bernoulli logit using the library GLM primitive. `X` is a finite numeric matrix of already transformed, commonly available covariates; `feature_order` gives distinct column names. An n-by-zero matrix fits the selection-rate intercept. `selected` is a binary mechanism indicator. `mechanism` is `decision`, `takeup`, `funding` or `outcome_observation`, and must equal `target.event`. Input rows must be the eligible risk set for that mechanism: for example uptake is conditional on an offer, not on every submitted application. Fit the mechanisms separately and predict their conditional probabilities for the target population before multiplying them.

Each `row_metadata` record requires `entity_id`, decision `as_of`, `features_known_at` and mechanism `label_known_at`. Features must be available at decision time. `folds` contains unique zero-based `train` and `validation` index arrays. Entities cannot cross train/validation within a fold; all training labels must strictly precede every validation decision date. Duplicate validation predictions are rejected. Early warmup observations receive null out-of-fold predictions, never in-sample replacements. The final common model is fitted to all declared training rows. `training` requires `known_at`, `split_id`, `data_fingerprint`, with optional `data_origin`; labels later than the cutoff are rejected. `model_id` and `model_version` identify the fitted artifact. Diagnostics include fold model hashes, OOF predictions, warmup indices and the minimum OOF probability. Rank deficiency and deterministic-policy separation fail explicitly. Fitting a propensity does not establish overlap, conditional MAR or transportability.

## Weights and training scenarios

`selection_ipw_weights(observed, propensities, *, target_weights=None, stabilization=None, trim=None, context=None)` accepts an observed-target indicator and either one propensity per row or a rows-by-mechanisms matrix of sequential conditional probabilities. Their product is e. `target_weights` are finite nonnegative population masses, default one. Observed retained rows receive `w/e`; unobserved rows receive zero. Optional `stabilization` supplies one marginal rate in (0,1] per mechanism, whose product multiplies all weights. This constant affects totals, not normalized means. `trim=[lower,upper]`, with lower>0, explicitly restricts the estimand to the included support; it does not silently winsorize e. Zero probability in positive retained target mass fails identification. The result reports support exclusions, changed estimand, retained/excluded mass and Kish ESS. Multiplication assumes sequential conditional exchangeability at every selection step, including outcome censoring when that is modeled.

`fuzzy_reject_augmentation(outcomes, observed, baseline_pd, *, application_ids, augmentation_weight=1, context=None)` retains each actual observed label with weight one. Every missing label becomes two hypothetical training rows with weights `augmentation_weight*(1-p)` and `augmentation_weight*p`; their mass sums exactly to the specified nonnegative scalar. `baseline_pd` must be frozen probabilities in [0,1] aligned to unique `application_ids`. Freeze and retain the originating model outside this calculation; this function neither fits nor validates it. Each generated row is `HYPOTHETICAL` and validation-ineligible. Fuzzy augmentation assumes the supplied missing-applicant risk, rather than discovering it from missing labels.

`parcel_rejected_applications(bands, accepted_defaults, accepted_counts, rejected_counts, *, log_odds_shift=0, uniforms=None, context=None)` uses unique frozen band names and integer cell counts. Accepted counts must be positive in every supplied band; unsupported bands must be handled explicitly before this calculation. Per-band reject risks are `expit(logit(defaults/counts)+shift)`, using a scalar or per-band shift. Exactly zero/one observed rates remain at those boundaries; no hidden smoothing prior is added. Without `uniforms`, output is fractional hypothetical bad mass. With a list of per-band explicit uniforms in [0,1), one draw per rejected record, sampled scenario labels are returned as well. No RNG is called. Zero-reject bands leave observed counts unchanged. Both forms are training scenarios, never independent target observations.

## Joint binary selection likelihood

`fit_joint_selection_model(X_outcome, X_selection, selected, outcomes, *, outcome_features, selection_features, target, training, model_id, model_version, rho_grid=None, selected_rho=None, exclusion=None, context=None)` fits bivariate probit, not an OLS inverse-Mills correction. Both finite matrices have the same rows. Their named feature lists are distinct within each design; a shared feature name must have exactly the same values. An intercept is fitted in both equations. `selected` marks observation of the actual own-contract target; unselected outcomes must remain missing. The common outcome target requires `conditioning="POTENTIAL_OWN_CONTRACT"` because prediction concerns the unconditional potential own-contract event. It is not a calibrated PD for observed funded customers.

Let a=Xβ, b=Zγ, and ρ be the standard-normal latent error correlation. Per-row likelihood contributions are Φ(-b) for S=0, Φ₂(a,b;ρ) for S=1,Y=1, and Φ₂(-a,b;-ρ) for S=1,Y=0. The deterministic Plackett correlation integral uses 64-point Gauss-Legendre quadrature in both native languages. Analytic scores drive bounded L-BFGS optimization initialized by genuine separate probit fits. Boundary, separation and convergence failures are controlled errors. Extremely tiny likelihood cells (≤1e-14) are excluded from the numerical optimizer's feasible region; the routine does not report their truncated log likelihood as a valid fit.

Two identification modes are explicit. A nonempty `rho_grid` in [-.95,.95] profiles fixed correlations, and `selected_rho` must explicitly choose one supplied scenario. The algorithm does not choose the most favorable scenario as validation. Alternatively, estimating ρ requires `exclusion={features: [...], justification: "..."}`. These named columns must occur only in the selection design and add independent design rank beyond the outcome design. Substantive exclusion validity remains a user assertion. Diagnostics contain every profile, its model-assumed mean potential-contract PD, score residuals, observed-information covariance, Hessian condition number and weak-identification flag. Fixed-ρ covariance is conditional on the supplied correlation and does not include its uncertainty. All fit inference is IID; it is not household-robust inference.

The artifact's `feature_order` is the outcome columns followed by selection-only columns. Generic `predict_pd` requires this exact order and target, and returns Φ(Xβ) from the frozen outcome coefficients. Diagnostics additionally report fitted selected and unselected conditional risks. Serialization preserves all model coefficients and assumptions for native cross-language prediction; solver iteration counts need not agree.

## Population means, partial identification and sensitivity

`estimate_population_risk_aipw(outcomes, observed, outcome_predictions, propensities, *, fold_lineage, target_weights=None, clusters=None, context=None)` computes the weighted mean of `m + S/e*(Y-m)`. `outcome_predictions` and `propensities` are [0,1] nuisance predictions, with positive e on positive target mass. `fold_lineage` contains `rows` and `folds` under the temporal/entity contract above, plus `nuisance_prediction_origin="OUT_OF_FOLD"`; this declaration applies to both nuisance predictions. Every positive target-weight row must have an OOF prediction. Assign zero target weight to warmup rows, making the resulting evaluation population explicit. Lineage declarations are audited but cannot prove how externally supplied numbers were trained.

`clusters` optionally supplies dependence-group IDs; by default entity IDs are used. A repeated entity cannot be split across clusters. At least two positive-mass independent clusters are required. The output includes observation influence contributions, cluster sandwich standard error and an untruncated normal 95% interval. This large-sample uncertainty requires adequate nuisance convergence and enough independent clusters. The estimate can fall outside [0,1], which is reported instead of silently clipping it. Double robustness is for a population mean under MAR/positivity, not individual declined-applicant PD or general MNAR identification.

`reject_inference_bounds(outcomes, observed, *, target_weights=None, strata=None, risk_bounds=None, context=None)` returns the sharp missing-binary-outcome interval `[observed default mass, observed default mass + missing mass]/total mass`. `strata` optionally gives one textual stratum per row. `risk_bounds` maps existing stratum names to explicit `[lower,upper]` restrictions on missing risk. Unspecified strata retain [0,1]. This also expresses an asserted monotonic-risk restriction, for example by supplying the observed stratum rate as a lower bound; it is an assumption, not an inferred property. Fully observed targets collapse to a point. The result exposes stratum contributions and its assumption hierarchy.

`reject_inference_sensitivity(baseline_pd, observed, outcomes, *, log_odds_shifts, target_weights=None, threshold=None, context=None)` holds observed outcomes fixed and shifts missing risks in log-odds space over the unique finite user grid. The baseline probabilities are frozen inputs, not estimates from hypothetical labels. The output separates observed mass from assumed missing mass and reports each population-risk scenario, boundary-probability count and optional `threshold` crossing brackets between adjacent supplied grid points. It does not interpolate a falsely precise identified crossover. Correlation sensitivity is separately available through the joint model's explicit fixed-rho profiles and their frozen outcome coefficients. Neither sensitivity surface is validation evidence.

## Evaluation evidence scopes

`prepare_reject_evaluation(candidates, evaluation_rows, *, as_of, target, outcome_comparability=None, context=None)` builds an evaluation-only SplitPlan with eligible and excluded zero-based indices. Every frozen candidate requires `model_id`, `model_hash`, `training_known_at`, and an explicit `training_entities` list (empty only if genuinely none). Every evaluation record requires unique `application_id`, `entity_id`, observation `as_of`, label `known_at`, and `label_origin`. Evaluation decisions must follow each candidate's training cutoff and entities must be disjoint from training. Violations are contamination flags and exclusion, not silently accepted tests.

`OBSERVED_OWN_CONTRACT` requires matching `target`, `funded=True`, `mature=True` and binary `outcome`. `AUTHORIZED_EXPLORATION` additionally requires a local `authorization_reference`; the API only reads evidence and never takes lending actions. `SYNTHETIC_TRUTH` requires matching target and `data_origin="SYNTHETIC"`, and is engineering-only evidence. All-synthetic results explicitly carry synthetic provenance. `EXTERNAL_CONTRACT` cannot validate the own-contract target without `outcome_comparability={target: ..., transport_assumption: "..."}`, and remains an explicitly assumed external-proxy scope even then. `HYPOTHETICAL`, `PSEUDO`, and `UNOBSERVED` rows never become eligible truth. A pseudo-only evaluation has `validation_identified=False`. No AUC or candidate ranking is computed from pseudo labels.

## Verification and references

Fast default tests use real small propensity/joint fits and independent hand calculations for sequential IPW, ESS, fractional mass, log-odds parcels, weighted bounds, AIPW cluster variance and date/entity guards. The bivariate-normal integral is checked against independent conditional-normal adaptive integration and the exact zero-threshold arcsine identity. Extended fixed-correlation profiles and larger estimated-correlation checks run only with `CREDITRATINGSYSTEM_EXTENDED_TESTS=true`. No test needs an internet connection or user data.

The treatment of unfunded outcomes, augmentation assumptions and invalid pseudo-label comparisons follows the missing-data analysis in [Ehrhardt et al., *Reject Inference Methods in Credit Scoring*](https://adimajo.github.io/assets/publications/rejectInference.pdf). The joint estimator uses the binary endogenous-probit likelihood described in the [native R endogenous sample-selection documentation](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/endogenous/html/sampselect.html), with its own deterministic quadrature implementation. Augmented inverse-probability estimation and its MAR conditions follow [Robins, Rotnitzky and Zhao (1994)](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476818). These references establish methods and assumptions, not the suitability of a bank's data, exclusions or selected model.
