# Seven sector model histories and dated local governance

Run `examples/wholesale_sector_governance.py`, or source the native R example and call `run_wholesale_sector_governance()`. Both accept `data`, `verify_reference` (true), `artifact_paths` (optional map of resource filename to local path) and `return_audit_artifacts` (false). The optional audit return includes the immutable registry, original requests and complete historical results, enabling independent cross-language replay. All references are synthetic, local and offline.

The seven histories bind actual fitted artifacts from the bank, life insurer, NAV-backed fund, foreign-currency sovereign, region, multilateral lender and housing-association component examples. Each uses that component's exact feature transformation and sealed holdout calibrator. No illustrative constant PD replaces the actual model. Packaged model envelopes are frozen once; they are not refitted during historical assessment. The companion component examples expose the local user fits and raw mathematical checks.

This governance scope is **calibrated standalone risk before support and transfer overlays**. Those effects and their economic targets remain explicit in the corresponding component examples; these histories do not claim to replay a different final supported or transfer-adjusted assessment. Seven product-bound rule sets require product, variant, borrower class, contractual rights and economic driver, with explicit feature freshness.

The March assessment is dated after its source financial information. A compiled policy combines calibrated PD with distinct synthetic unrestricted cashflow/debt-service gates: bank 150/100, insurer 100/110, fund 50/45, sovereign 120/130, region 75/60, supranational 100/70 and housing 40/45. Those cash budgets are separately declared new facility credit-review inputs; they do not reuse restricted balances as spendable cash. The insurer, sovereign and housing applications receive REFER, while the others satisfy the specified APPROVE rule. These are local scenario decisions, not funding actions. Finalizing a rating is distinct from approving or executing a loan.

A subsequent service ledger records a missed March payment, 31-day delinquency, an unexecuted restructuring proposal, observed May default and September legal settlement. Seven distinct scheduled claims and recovery amounts are retained in the resource. A duplicated review event is applied once. Rating review/finalization and claim exit transition DRAFT → REVIEWED → FINAL → SUPERSEDED; legal claim settlement is not inferred to cure the obligor. The history retains the original model, calibration, information cutoff and risk result. A later genuinely fitted alternative model cannot replace it. Tampering with a historical event-state seal produces NOT_REPRODUCIBLE.

The full Python and native audit payloads agree, including model, input, recipe, assessment and event hashes; both foreign histories reproduce through the other runtime. Default package tests exercise one complete real history, including the subsequent real GLM fit and replay controls. `CREDITRATINGSYSTEM_EXTENDED_TESTS=true` exercises all seven. Observation-panel estimation, workout estimation and portfolio monitoring are separate phase evidence, not inferred merely from this event log.

The reusable engine accepts optional per-case `timeline` overrides, `subject_type`, `reference_action` and `calibrator_arguments` (`groups` only). Timeline fields are `routing_start`, `origination`, `processing`, `review`, `finalize`, `performing_review`, `payment_due`, `warning`, `warning_known`, `delinquency`, `delinquency_known`, `proposal`, `default`, `default_known`, `closure`, `replay`, `later_registration`, and `later_replay`. Defaults reproduce the original seven full audit payloads exactly. These are explicit dates, not an automatic time shift of source model training, policy eligibility or financial observations; callers must supply those consistently. Occurrence/knowledge and lifecycle chronology are checked. Days past due derive from `payment_due` and `delinquency`.
