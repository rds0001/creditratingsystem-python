# Observed sector cohorts and legally closed workouts

Run `examples/wholesale_sector_observed.py`, or source the native R example and call `run_wholesale_sector_observed()`. Python accepts `data`, `verify_reference` (true) and `artifact_paths` (optional filename/path map); R additionally accepts `engine_path` for an explicit shared example path. This example reuses the tested `specialized_observed_lifecycle` calculation engine, with an explicit cohort contract instead of copying its financial mathematics. The seven frozen model resources come from `wholesale_sector_governance`.

The bank, life insurer, NAV-backed fund, foreign-currency sovereign, region, multilateral lender and housing-association cohorts each contain 22 separately observed contracts. Twenty have complete follow-up; one loses coverage and one exits before the outcome horizon. The active zero-drawn-balance contract remains eligible. Distinct default counts are 1, 2, 3, 2, 1, 1 and 2. Labels come from dated servicing events, never simulation outcomes or model pseudo-labels. No historical approval decision is invented for these booked contracts.

Each homogeneous synthetic risk cohort receives its representative component's actual frozen-model forecast on 1 March 2024. The wrapper loads and validates the model envelope, uses its preserved transformed design row and applies the sealed calibrator, checks the model's knowledge cutoff, and verifies every stored prediction against the result. Altering a forecast fails even if reference assertions are disabled. This is conditional reference-cohort evidence; it does not claim that real bank customers share identical risk or that independent binomial uncertainty is appropriate for dependent real portfolios. The supplied synthetic cohorts explicitly use independent entities and homogeneous forecast risk.

The 365-day horizon ends 1 March 2025, with evaluation on 2 March. No 2024 financial feature is backdated into a 2023 forecast. Model and calibration identities, actual source design rows and target are explicit. The sovereign cohort's first credit defaults correspond to its pre-transfer credit target; transfer-only events are not relabeled as credit defaults. Monetary recovery remains USD for that case and EUR for the other six, without an invented FX conversion.

Observed default is 1 May 2024. Net settlement on 1 September is discounted over exactly 123 actual/365 days at the declared 5% annual rate. For each sector, the independent oracle is `(gross recovery-direct cost)/1.05**(123/365)`, divided by observed default EAD to derive LGD. Legal settlement and residual write-off close the workout on 3 September, known on 4 September; this closure is not cure. The same incomplete cash record marked OPEN has no final economic LGD. Open-workout estimation is separate remaining phase evidence.

Default counts, Brier scores, censoring, cashflow PV and closed LGD are independently checked for all seven cases. The shared engine's original five specialized results remain exactly unchanged in both languages. The additive `cohort_contract` specifies forecast date/knowledge date, horizon, outcome knowledge date, evaluation cutoff, reference window, total/mature/censored counts and reporting currency; a workout can explicitly override its currency. Existing defaults preserve the original specialized reference histories.
