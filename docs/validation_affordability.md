# Frozen household affordability validation

`affordability_stress_backtest(cases, economic_evidence, allocations, design=..., payment_outcomes=None, ...)` verifies existing frozen `stress_affordability` assessments against dated source evidence and household allocations. It reruns the actual contractual stress engine; it does not build a substitute scorecard or infer default probability from budget surplus.

The lifecycle and creditworthiness context is described by the [EBA loan origination and monitoring guidelines](https://www.eba.europa.eu/activities/single-rulebook/regulatory-activities/credit-risk/guidelines-loan-origination-and-monitoring). The following interfaces and arithmetic are explicit library contracts, not a claim of comprehensive current legal compliance.

Each case carries the common dated validation identities and `household_id`, the actual `baseline` and `scenario` accepted by `stress_affordability`, its frozen `assessment` payload, `scenario_known_at`, `profile_known_at`, `profile_version`, `evidence_bindings`, `expected_economic_ids`. Baseline currency/as-of and final projection date must match the design and forecast horizon. Scenario assumptions and threshold profiles must be historically known. Expected IDs explicitly list all required incomes, expenses, contracts and collateral; unknown income is not represented by silently deleting its required source.

Economic evidence has unique `evidence_id`, `economic_id`, `kind` (incomes, expenses, contracts, collateral), `reference`, `observed_at`, `known_at`, Boolean `verified`, and `terms`. Terms correspond to a baseline economic record before household allocation, omitting its economic ID. Income terms use normalized monthly net `monthly_amount` and `basis='net'`; evidence additionally names original `source_basis` net or gross. Gross sources provide `monthly_gross_amount`, `monthly_tax_and_deductions`, `normalization_reference`; gross less deductions must reconcile to net. Amounts in other frequencies must be explicitly normalized upstream. Contract terms retain maturity, amortization, balloon, fixed/reset coupon dates, floors and caps; collateral and expenses retain their own currencies.

Allocation records have unique `allocation_id`, `economic_id`, `household_id`, `as_of`, `known_at`, `share`, `mode`. A PROPORTIONAL source share is between zero and one; shares across distinct households at the same date cannot exceed one. FULL_RECOURSE is allowed only for contracts and requires share one. Such a liability can appear in more than one obligor's affordability test under the explicit recourse assumption; assessments are not automatically aggregated as distinct cashflows. Repeated scenarios for one household reuse the same dated allocation record. Income/expense amounts, collateral value, and contract principal/balloon amounts are checked against the allocated source terms. The same economic source cannot quietly appear in two budget groups.

Missing, unverified or late-known evidence blocks a verified projection. Gross/net or shared-allocation conflicts block the evidence set. The frozen assessment is compared recursively with a genuine stress rerun, including reset timing, FX, repayment, collateral and policy thresholds. Numeric comparisons use the supplied tolerance; dates, types, identities and categorical decisions remain exact. The output retains recomputed conditional projections and differing paths, and marks whether each projection is verified.

Optional factual payment records match `prediction_id` and `period_date`; they contain currency, `scheduled_payment`, `paid_amount`, `known_at`, `observed_through` and Boolean `complete`. Only mature known periods with complete payment capture are scored. Missing/incomplete capture is not zero payment or nondefault. The descriptive metric is actual scheduled payment minus actual paid amount, with optional shared whole-case-cluster bootstrap intervals. Scenario debt service is shown alongside it but is never presumed to be the realized schedule; no stress-scenario accuracy or causal effect is inferred from that comparison.

For example, verified gross monthly income250 less deductions50 gives net200. Expenses50 and a1000 interest-only balance at a reset coupon of24% imply monthly debt service20 and residual130. A fixed12% contract instead has service10 and residual140 under the same rate-shock input. If the factual schedule was10 and actual payment8, the separate observed payment shortfall is2. None of these arithmetic results means PD is zero or that credit must be approved.
