# Sector competing exits, conditional treatment and open claims

Run `examples/wholesale_sector_risk_review.py` or call native `run_wholesale_sector_risk_review()`. Both accept `data` and `verify_reference` (true); R additionally accepts `engine_paths` for explicit shared example paths. The example reuses the actual fitting engines in `specialized_risk_review` and `specialized_open_workout`. Seven product-specific data sets cover bank, insurer, fund, sovereign, regional government, supranational lender and housing association.

The cause-specific models fit historical 20-entity risk sets, with distinct default/competing-exit counts. Baseline-only Breslow estimates have independent cumulative-incidence oracle: with d defaults and e exits at year one, `h=(d+e)/20`, total event mass is `1-exp(-h)` and default/exit shares are d/(d+e) and e/(d+e). Competing exits are not counted as nondefaults over an otherwise fully observed horizon.

Fourteen proposed arrangements use separately declared prospective cash projections. Named options address funding runoff, insurance portfolio transfer, fund asset sale, sovereign exchange, budget repayment, member calls and service continuity. Each is compared with immediate claim settlement. Expectations are not copied from the later realized settlement ledger. Each candidate specifies uptake, offer and acceptance costs, rejection value and dated recovery/cost cashflows. The independent value is uptake × (discounted net accepted cash minus upfront cost) plus nonuptake × rejection value minus offer cost. These are conditional scenarios without causal treatment-effect identification; no offer is executed. Candidate IDs, not array order, bind the numerical oracle.

Seven actual hurdle models fit independent historical workout records: four cures, four zero residual payouts, four positive payouts and four censored workouts per sector. Positive log payout ratios are `log(scale)+[-0.3,-0.1,0.1,0.3]`, yielding variance 0.05; scales are 0.65, 0.50, 0.40, 0.35, 0.60, 0.80 and 0.55. Censored Weibull resolution timing is estimated rather than replaced by a fixed delay. Cure probability 1/3 and positive-given-no-cure probability 1/2 imply ultimate expected LGD `1-(1+scale*exp(0.025))/3`.

The June 2024 open snapshots follow observed May default but contain no interim recovery and no later September settlement information. Residual exposure equals default EAD. The declared flat zero-discount curve and explicit ultimate tail make the financial oracle independent of timing-grid truncation; timing-grid acceptance is not claimed here. Sovereign amounts remain USD and the other amounts EUR. This valuation convention differs from the observed five-percent closed-workout ledger; the example does not mix the two conventions in a backtest. All raw historical observations, target contracts and fitted stage assumptions are supplied in the packaged JSON.

Default tests execute all seven real competing-exit and hurdle fits, financial/event oracles, and a reordered-candidate regression. Full Python/native financial and treatment results agree within the common strict numerical tolerance, with exact seals. Portfolio monitoring and longitudinal drift remain separate phase evidence.
