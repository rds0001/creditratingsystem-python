# Macro dynamics, cycle distributions and satellite transmission

The eleven functions in `creditratingsystem.macro` estimate and apply joint macroeconomic dynamics. Native R exposes the same function names and portable fitted artifacts. Estimation uses only declared training observations; simulation consumes explicit shared innovations. A finite sample average of a forecast is not an estimate of an unconditional through-the-cycle law.

## Data and information contracts

`transform_macro_history(history, variable_transforms, frequency, fit_window, information_cutoff)` takes long records with `variable_id`, economic `date`, `release_date` and numeric `value`. A transform record specifies `variable_id`, `transform` (`identity`, `log`, `difference`, `log_difference`), boolean `standardize`, and original `unit`. Frequency is `monthly`, `quarterly` or `annual`. The fit window contains ISO `start`, `end`, and `known_at`; the separate information cutoff controls the output vintage. Transformation centers and population standard deviations use the training vintage available at `known_at`. Later revisions can change output history but cannot change frozen training values. Log transforms require positive levels. There is no interpolation or implicit conversion of rates into levels.

The returned `MacroHistory` has ordered `variable_ids`, regular `dates`, row-major `values`, per-cell `release_dates`, `units`, `frequency`, training vintage and inverse transformation state. Differencing removes the first aligned row across all variables. Inversion retains the original training endpoint, not a later revised endpoint. Estimators also accept this wide history shape directly. Each fit requires `training` with `start`, `end`, `known_at`, `split_id` and optional `data_origin`; the actual training fingerprint is computed. `target` declares `event`, positive `horizon`, `time_unit`, `probability_measure` (`PHYSICAL` or `RISK_NEUTRAL`) and `conditioning`. `model_id` and `model_version` are explicit identifiers. Optional `context` cannot contradict the fitted probability measure.

## VAR and cointegration

`fit_var(transformed_history, lags, deterministic="constant", covariance_estimator="df_adjusted", training, target, model_id, model_version, context=None)` fits joint OLS dynamics. Deterministic terms are `none`, `constant`, `trend`, or `constant_trend`; the trend is the original training observation index starting at one. Innovation covariance uses divisor effective sample size for `mle` or residual degrees of freedom for `df_adjusted`. Multivariate implementations use statsmodels VAR and native R vars; univariate equations use their OLS counterpart. Positive residual degrees of freedom and an identifiable design are required. Companion eigenvalues and stability are reported without changing estimated dynamics.

`fit_vecm(level_history, rank, lags, deterministic="constant_inside", integration_order="I1", training, target, model_id, model_version, context=None)` estimates a Gaussian reduced-rank cointegration model. `lags` counts levels lags, so there are `lags-1` differenced lags. `rank` is explicitly selected between zero and dimension minus one. The integration assumption is declared, not established by this fit. Already differenced transformation histories are rejected. Supported terms are `none`, `constant_inside`, `constant_outside`, `constant_outside_trend_inside`, and `constant_trend_outside`. Inside terms enter the cointegrating relation; outside terms enter the differenced equation. Cointegrating vectors are normalized to an identity leading block; an unsuitable normalization fails. Rank-zero estimates have no error correction.

Python uses statsmodels VECM. Native R uses urca Johansen estimation where its parameterization matches, and the same residual covariance generalized-eigenvalue likelihood for the remaining deterministic/one-lag cases. Both retain alpha, beta, short-run coefficients, eigenvalue/trace diagnostics and a common levels recursion. Trace statistics do not automatically select a rank or invent critical values. Unit roots remain visible and exclude stationary Gaussian TTC initialization.

## Hidden regimes

`fit_regime_switching(transformed_history, regime_count, lags=0, initialization, max_iterations=200, tolerance=1e-8, training, target, model_id, model_version, context=None)` estimates a multivariate Gaussian autoregressive hidden Markov model by EM. The emission is an intercept plus regime-specific coefficients multiplying observed lagged levels, with a full covariance per regime. This is not a regression on a preassigned recession indicator, nor an autoregression centered on historical latent regime means.

`initialization` is a nonempty list of explicit starts. Each has `transition` (origin rows, destination columns), `initial_probabilities`, `coefficients` of shape regime × (one intercept plus variable-major blocks within consecutive lags) × response, and `covariances` of shape regime × response × response. All starts run; the largest observed-data likelihood is retained, with failures and convergence traces preserved. Covariance collapse or insufficient weighted occupancy fails without a hidden ridge. Regimes are ordered lexicographically by intercept. Filtered probabilities use information through their date; smoothed probabilities use the entire training sample and are labeled accordingly. EM may reach a local optimum or its iteration limit; diagnostics make no global claim.

## Dynamic factors

`fit_dynamic_factor(transformed_history, factor_count, initialization, ar_limit=.995, variance_bounds=(1e-8,1e4), max_iterations=300, tolerance=1e-8, training, target, model_id, model_version, context=None)` fits a Gaussian state-space model by actual Kalman likelihood: observations equal means plus factor loadings times latent factors plus idiosyncratic states. Factors and idiosyncratic states each follow their own AR(1), with independent positive Gaussian innovations. There are fewer factors than observed variables. The first factor-count loading rows are lower triangular with diagonal one; this fixes scale, sign and orientation. The bounds are declared estimation constraints, not post-fit repairs.

Initialization contains `loadings`, `mean`, `ar` and `variances`. State order is factors first, then one idiosyncratic state per observed variable. Missing observed cells are integrated out through the observed-subvector likelihood; they are not interpolated. Python uses statsmodels KalmanFilter and SciPy optimization; native R uses KFAS and stats optimization. Portable artifacts contain observation, transition and state innovation covariance matrices, the terminal filtered distribution and identification diagnostics. The reported optimizer status must be assessed before model use; returning an inspected fit at an explicit iteration cap does not assert convergence. Factors are not replaced by static PCA scores.

## Shared innovations and ScenarioCube

`block_bootstrap_scenarios(residual_history, shared_draws, factor_ids, scenario_ids, time_grid, frequency, block_length, scheme="moving", weights=None, context=None)` resamples whole multivariate residual rows. The supplied uniforms have shape scenario × time × two: block start and restart. Moving blocks use nonwrapping starts; circular blocks wrap; stationary blocks restart with probability `1/block_length`. Deterministic block lengths are integers, stationary lengths are expected lengths. Selected zero-based row indices and block IDs are returned. Output is `RAW_INNOVATIONS`, not a Gaussian distribution.

`simulate_macro_scenarios(fitted_model, innovations, initial_state=None, regime_uniforms=None, output_space="model", sampling_contract=None, context=None)` propagates a frozen macro model. The innovation object contains `kind` (`STANDARD_NORMAL` or, for linear dynamics, `RAW_INNOVATIONS`), numeric `values` on scenario/time/factor axes, ordered `scenario_ids` and `factor_ids`, `time_grid` in years and optional normalized `weights`. Time steps must match consecutive frequency periods. For factors, input channel IDs are the artifact's latent `state_ids`; output factor IDs are the observed macro variables. Standard normals are mapped through a correlation-standardized PSD factor. Numerical negative correlation eigenvalues within a declared rounding tolerance are reported; materially invalid covariances and negative variances fail. No independent macro histories are generated for separate counterparties.

Output `ScenarioCube` includes schema version, named axes and IDs, values, weights, units, frequency, `time_unit="years"`, economic `origin`, information cutoff, probability measure, model hash, data origin and full sampling contract. The same named scenarios should flow through downstream exposure, PD, LGD, collateral and loss calculations.

### PIT and TTC initialization

Linear VAR/VECM defaults to `FORECAST_CONDITIONAL`: the last observed history, or a declared fixed history of shape lags × variables, determines a PIT forecast. A stationary claim requires an explicit `STATIONARY_GAUSSIAN` sampling contract with `cycle_law_id`, `equilibrium_assumptions` and normal `initial_state_draws` of shape scenario × companion dimension. The stable constant-coefficient system's equilibrium mean and Lyapunov covariance determine the initial law. Companion state draws order the most recent observation first. Deterministic trends, unit roots and explosive roots disallow this law. The simulation does not obtain equilibrium by averaging a short conditional forecast.

`DECLARED_CYCLE_MIXTURE` instead takes scenario-specific `initial_state` histories, `phase_ids`, `phase_probabilities`, cycle-law identifier and assumptions. Scenario weight mass must equal each declared phase probability. The artifact explicitly records that the economic equilibrium justification was supplied and was not statistically verified by this function.

Regime simulation additionally requires uniforms of shape scenario × (one initial draw plus forecast steps). Its default samples the terminal filtered regime posterior, then transitions each forecast period. `STATIONARY_MARKOV_EMISSION` permits a unique stationary regime distribution only for zero observed lags, with explicit cycle-law assumptions. Switching autoregressive dynamics do not acquire a joint invariant law merely by solving stationary regime probabilities.

Dynamic factors default to `FILTERED_GAUSSIAN`, requiring initial normal state draws in the sampling contract; filtered state uncertainty is retained. `DECLARED_FIXED_STATE` explicitly conditions on a supplied state vector. Stable `STATIONARY_GAUSSIAN` initialization uses the unconditional latent covariance and explicit cycle-law assumptions and initial normal draws.

`output_space="level"` reverses frozen transformations with fitted original-level anchors. Alternative initial/cycle laws require separate original-level anchoring and are rejected by this convenience inversion. Their model-space output remains available for explicitly constructed economic level scenarios.

## Conditional and reverse stress

`condition_macro_paths(fitted_model, innovations, conditioning_points, initial_state=None, sampler="gaussian_equality", sampling_contract=None, context=None)` conditions the full joint Gaussian path. Points identify zero-based `time_index`, `factor_id` and exact `value`. It applies the cross-covariance conditional mean/covariance and adjusts all correlated path coordinates. Standardized covariance support determines feasibility; a deterministic contradiction fails. Empty constraints return the same unconditional cube. Gaussian conditioning requires standard-normal innovations; an empirical bootstrap or hidden-regime/cycle mixture is not silently substituted with a Gaussian law. Latent and stationary initial uncertainty enters the covariance.

The output records `CONDITIONAL_STRESS` and the parent PIT/TTC scope. A constraint density is not the probability of an exact continuous event. Full-rank density is expressed in physical constraint coordinates; singular-support density uses standardized constraint coordinates and is labeled accordingly.

`reverse_stress_scenario(fitted_model, loss_model, horizon, target_loss, shock_metric, initial_state=None, shock_bounds=None, initial_shock=None, tolerance=1e-7, max_iterations=500, context=None)` seeks a minimum half-quadratic innovation shock attaining a response threshold for linear VAR/VECM dynamics. The metric is an explicit positive definite precision matrix on flattened time-major standard-normal shocks. Bounds have one lower/upper pair per shock. The response is either a callable accepting a horizon × factor path, or a portable `linear_predictor` object with same-shaped `coefficients`, scalar `intercept` and `identity`, `logit` or `log` link. An augmented-Lagrangian constrained search reports feasibility, optimizer status, achieved response, shock and objective. A local feasible point is not proof of the global minimum; failure to find one is not generally proof of infeasibility.

## Satellites

`fit_satellite_model(target_series, macro_history, feature_spec, estimator, link, split_plan, training, target, target_unit, model_id, model_version, weights=None, covariance="classical", hac_lags=0, residual_process="white_noise", context=None)` fits released macro predictors to dated observed targets. Each target record has `observation_id`, `entity_id`, `date`, `information_date`, `known_at` and `value`. A feature declares `feature_id`, `variable_id`, nonnegative integer `lag`, physical `unit` and `expected_sign` (`positive`, `negative`, `unrestricted`). Sign hypotheses are diagnosed, not imposed as hidden constraints.

The SplitPlan declares matching `split_id`, `training_cutoff`, `mode` (`TEMPORAL` or `ENTITY_TIME`) and exhaustive disjoint `train_ids`, optional `calibration_ids`, `validation_ids`, `test_ids`. Nonempty holdout roles follow training and model availability strictly in time; entity-time mode also requires disjoint entity groups. Training target maturity and every lagged macro release must be known at the declared information dates. Holdout target values are not read for estimation.

OLS and WLS use identity or log-response transforms. WLS weights map exactly training observation IDs to positive precision weights. Classical, HC1, entity-cluster and single-series Bartlett HAC coefficient covariance are available; the latter requires explicit `hac_lags`. A log inverse without simulated residual draws is a conditional median, not a mean corrected by an invented variance. Binary GLM supports logit, probit and cloglog with integer frequency weights, Fisher covariance and `residual_process="none"`. It uses the established binary MLE's rank, separation and convergence guards.

Continuous residual processes are `none`, `white_noise` or fitted zero-intercept `AR1`. AR1 pairs must be consecutive within the same entity; gaps and entity boundaries are not stitched. Nonstationary estimates fail. Residual innovation scale is estimated explicitly from the respective residual innovations.

`apply_satellite_model(fitted_satellite_model, scenario_cube, idiosyncratic_innovations, initial_target_state, context=None)` accepts one shared macro cube and a `STANDARD_NORMAL` idiosyncratic object with exactly matching scenario IDs/time grid and scenario × time values. Initial target state declares common `as_of`, `known_at`, scalar link-scale `residual_state`, and `mode`. Initial `macro_history` covers exactly the maximum macro lag and ends at the cube origin. `absolute` applies the fitted intercept and lagged drivers. `baseline_delta` adds fitted changes relative to `reference_feature_path` onto an explicit `baseline_link_path`; equal driver/reference paths and zero residual leave the baseline unchanged. Output preserves macro sampling metadata and exposes baseline, macro and residual link contributions for reconstruction.

## Evidence and primary references

Default tests include independent normal-equation regressions, rank-zero difference equations, Gaussian path conditioning, stationary AR(1) covariance, finite hidden-state enumeration, dense joint-Gaussian likelihood/posterior checks with missing entries, and satellite lag/covariance/leakage oracles. Expensive deterministic VECM grids are enabled with `CREDITRATINGSYSTEM_EXTENDED_TESTS=true`. Python/R frozen artifacts are replayed in both directions with the same supplied draws; matching a random seed alone is not cross-language evidence.

Backend parameter conventions are documented by [statsmodels VAR/VECM](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/vector_ar.html), [vars VAR](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/vars/html/VAR.html), [urca Johansen estimation](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/urca/html/ca.jo.html), and [KFAS state-space models](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/KFAS/html/SSModel.html). The distinctions between filtered and smoothed regimes and between static and dynamic factors are also illustrated in the primary [statsmodels regime](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/examples/notebooks/generated/markov_autoregression.html) and [dynamic-factor](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/examples/notebooks/generated/statespace_dfm_coincident.html) documentation.
